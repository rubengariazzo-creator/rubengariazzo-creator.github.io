Icarus is an experimental mini-rocket designed, built, and flown to a nominal flight at C'Space 2026, the student launch campaign organized by CNES and Planète Sciences at the Ger military camp, hosted by the 1er RHP.

The project was carried out by a five-person team within EPF Astronomie, EPF's aerospace club: Camille Gaudeaux (project lead), Margaux Vahlas, Chimène Tabaste, Ambroise Denduang Wolber, and myself.

## My contribution

- **Nose cone design**: CAD modeling of a 210 mm ogive sized for an 83 mm airframe diameter.
- **Rapid prototyping and manufacturing**: 3D printing of key structural parts, including the fin mounting cage and the motor retaining rings.
- **Flight dynamics**: stability and trajectory simulations confirming Icarus would hold a straight, nominal flight path rather than tumbling.
- **Technical communication**: designed the project's 3D animation in Blender, kept under wraps until the back-to-school reveal.

## Stability and trajectory

Flight stability was validated with dedicated simulations modelling the rocket's trajectory and aerodynamic behavior based on its geometry and mass: a static margin of 6.2 to 6.5 calibers (overstable verdict), apogee at 230 m after 6.7 s, full flight in 31 s. Detailed results are available below.

## The flight

The launch was a success, with a nominal flight confirmed by an official flight certificate.
